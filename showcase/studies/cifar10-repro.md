## Verdict

We asked whether a careful, step-by-step re-implementation of Artificial Structure Function Search (ASF-S) reproduces the paper's result for Conv2Net on CIFAR-10. Every step up to and including pruning matched the paper, but the headline result did not reproduce.

The paper reports that after removing 75.8% of the parameters and retraining only the final fully connected (FC) layer, accuracy falls by 3.78 points (Table 5). At exactly the same operating point:
- Our best selection rule loses 10.50 points of test accuracy (mean of three fine-tuning seeds, standard deviation 0.05).
- The authors' literal Principal Gradient Importance (PGI) formula loses 15.00 points, which is no better than picking filters at random (−15.07).

The hypothesis did not hold. When we fine-tune the whole network instead of only the FC layer, we come much closer to the paper (−5.00 against −3.78). So we think the paper's Table 5 number was most likely produced with full fine-tuning.

## Why we think this happened

We have ruled out several explanations:
- **The architecture** is exactly the paper's: step 0 matches both the parameter and the FLOP counts.
- **The data augmentation** matches the authors' torchvision pipeline. A statistical comparison over 2,000 images found no difference in augmentation strength.
- **The FC-only learning rate** is not the cause. Rates of 1e-3 and 3e-3 made every method worse than the original 1e-4, and the ranking of methods stayed the same.
- **Updating the batch-normalisation statistics** during FC-only fine-tuning is not the cause either. Freezing them changed results by about ±1 point, with no consistent direction.
- **Seed noise** is far too small to explain a gap of 6.7 points. The three FC-only seeds of corrected PGI span only 0.09 points.

What we suspect, in decreasing order of confidence:

- **Table 5 was produced with full fine-tuning.** The paper does not say which fine-tuning mode Table 5 uses. The authors' original pruning script fine-tunes the whole model. Our full fine-tuning results for the non-random rules (−4.8 to −5.6) fall within about 1 to 2 points of −3.78. Read that way, Table 5 roughly reproduces, and only the FC-only claim does not.
- **Our dense model is stronger than the paper's.** Our Conv2Net reaches 75.32% against the paper's 72.69%, and four training runs landed between 74.8% and 76.5%. A stronger or weaker starting model may prune differently. We did not run the planned check with a model near 72.7%.
- **The method as written is ambiguous in places,** and the original code resolves the ambiguity in ways that weaken it.
  - The kernel width σ = 0.1 used in the original scripts makes the AFC graph fall apart into disconnected pieces, so PG1 is not uniquely defined. We used a median rule (σ = 0.831) instead.
  - Equation 12 multiplies vectors of different lengths. The original code truncates them, and its filter ranking is essentially uncorrelated with our dimensionally consistent reading (Spearman ρ of −0.16 and +0.03 for the two layers).
  - We have not run the fully original pipeline end to end, so we cannot say how much each of these matters for the final number.

We would change our mind in either of two cases:
- the paper's authors confirmed that Table 5 used FC-only fine-tuning with a setting we have not tried, or
- the fully original pipeline reached about −3.78 with FC-only fine-tuning.

## Limits

- We tested one architecture (Conv2Net) on one dataset (CIFAR-10). We did not attempt Conv6Net or the step 9 baselines from Table 5.
- All numbers use the "paper" protocol, where the test set is also used for early stopping and checkpoint selection, so they are optimistic. We never ran the "clean" protocol, which holds out a separate validation set.
- The three seeds vary only the fine-tuning. The dense model and the selected filters are the same for every seed, so the numbers leave out the variance from retraining the dense model or redrawing the PG1 images. The 49-point grid and the learning-rate check used a single seed.
- Two planned fairness checks were not run: the fully original pipeline end to end, and a weaker dense model near the paper's 72.7%.

## What this means for the project

We stop trying to reproduce the FC-only recovery claim, and treat it as not reproduced on Conv2Net. Two results carry forward:
1. The corrected (aligned) PGI is the only PGI variant that beats simple magnitude scores, and only under FC-only fine-tuning, where it beats L1 and L2 by about 1.5 points. Under full fine-tuning, all non-random rules are within seed noise of each other.
2. The authors' literal formula behaves like random selection.

These two points motivate Workstream 2. It asks, under a careful evaluation protocol, whether preserving functional connectivity selects better filters than simple scores, and it starts from our corrected PGI rather than the original formula. The detailed reasoning and every run are in this study's [lab log](https://github.com/NeuroPulse-Co/ASF-S/blob/main/studies/cifar10-repro/LOG.md).
